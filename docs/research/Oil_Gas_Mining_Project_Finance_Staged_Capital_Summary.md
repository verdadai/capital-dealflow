## 2. Oil & Gas Project Phases and Feasibility

Oil & gas (especially upstream and major midstream/LNG) projects follow staged, gated processes to manage technical, commercial, and capital risk. Terminology varies (Appraise/Select/Define/Execute/Operate; FEL 1-3; etc.).

### Typical Upstream / Field Development Lifecycle
1. **Exploration** — Geological/geophysical surveys, seismic, exploratory drilling.
2. **Appraisal / Delineation** — Further wells and studies to size the resource and assess commercial viability.
3. **Development planning / Concept selection** — Evaluate development options.
4. **Feasibility / Pre-FEED (often FEL-1 / Conceptual)** — High-level technical, economic, legal, operational, and scheduling assessment. Rough costs and go/no-go.
5. **FEED / Front-End Engineering Design (FEL-2/3 or Define)** — Detailed process design, equipment sizing, P&IDs, cost estimates (tighter accuracy, e.g., ±10-15% late FEED), schedule, contracting strategy, HSE/HAZOP, permitting. Scope freezes. Prepares for FID.
6. **FID (Final Investment Decision)** — Sanction to proceed with full funding and execution. Commits major capital. Triggers EPC (or EPCC).
7. **Execution / Construction** — Detailed engineering, procurement of long-lead items, construction, commissioning.
8. **Production / Operations** — Ramp-up to plateau, then decline management.
9. **Decommissioning / Abandonment**.

**Key feasibility elements**: Economic, technical, legal/regulatory, operational, scheduling. Bankable studies support financing. Cost accuracy improves across stages; early phases have wide ranges (±30-50%). Many projects experience subsurface surprises or cost/schedule overruns post-FID.

**Capital context**: Mega-projects often use consortium financing (lead arrangers + syndication), equity from sponsors/PE/infrastructure funds, RBL for producing assets, volumetric production payments, private credit, and ECA/DFI support. Recent dynamics include capital discipline, private credit growth, and selective bank appetite.

## 3. Staged Multi-Source Financing Across the Lifecycle

**Yes — deals are routinely structured so projects raise money from multiple sources at different phases.**

Early stages have high risk and limited cash flow → equity-heavy. As the project de-risks (especially post-FID), debt capacity rises. Construction involves large multi-year outlays that are often phased, so facilities use milestone-tied drawdowns. Large projects use multi-sourced structures (banks + ECAs + DFIs + capital markets + private credit) to reach quantum, diversify, and match phased spending.

### Typical Capital Sources by Phase
| Phase | Dominant Capital Sources | Notes |
|-------|---------------------------|-------|
| **Exploration / early appraisal** | Sponsor equity, JOA cash calls, farm-outs/farm-ins, carried interests, private equity or strategic partners | Highest risk; limited or no traditional debt. |
| **Late appraisal / concept selection / pre-FEED / FEED** | Additional equity, bridge facilities, mezzanine, some private credit | Bridges gaps until better terms available once reserves/design clearer. |
| **FID and construction / development** | Multi-sourced project finance (senior bank debt, ECA-backed loans, bonds/private placements, DFIs), more equity, hybrid instruments | Debt often in tranches released against construction milestones or phase progress. Gearing can reach 70%+. |
| **Production / operations** | Reserve-based lending (RBL), volumetric production payments (VPPs), prepayments from offtakers/traders, operating cash flow, refinancing | RBL can cover portfolios of assets at mixed stages. Cash flow becomes primary repayment source. |
| **Later field phases or expansions** | Sequential FIDs with new or incremental financing packages; holdco or portfolio-level facilities | Phased field development common; each major phase can have its own financing or linked tranches. |

### Key Structural Features
- **Tranches and phased drawdowns**: Debt availability linked to staged development. Intercreditor arrangements coordinate lenders.
- **Bridge-to-project or bridge-to-better-terms**: Short-term facilities fill gaps until larger/cheaper long-term debt is available.
- **Hybrid and alternative sources**: Trader prepayments, streams/royalties, VPPs, mezzanine with equity kickers, private credit.
- **Full funding at financial close**: Overall plan (equity + committed debt) locked in so the project is fully financed through completion.
- **Risk allocation**: Early equity absorbs subsurface/concept risk; later debt focuses on completion, offtake, and operating risks.

This is standard for both traditional upstream and larger midstream/LNG projects. Exact mixes vary by basin, jurisdiction, sponsor strength, prices, and scale.

## 4. Public Examples of Staged / Multi-Source Financing

### Oil & Gas / LNG / Drilling-Related Projects

**Ichthys LNG Project (Australia, INPEX-led with TotalEnergies and others)**  
- Long exploration/appraisal history (discoveries confirmed years earlier; further campaigns 2003–2007).  
- FEED started ~2009.  
- FID: January 2012.  
- Project finance financial close: December 2012 — ~US$20 billion multi-sourced package:  
  - ~US$5.8bn direct ECA loans (largest JBIC commitment at the time, plus KEXIM, EFIC, etc.).  
  - ~US$5.4bn ECA-covered/insured commercial bank loans.  
  - ~US$4.8bn uncovered commercial bank tranche (syndicate of 20+ banks).  
  - ~US$4bn senior sponsor/shareholder loans.  
- Construction phase followed; first LNG production/shipments 2018.  
- Financial completion ~2019; subsequent refinancings (e.g., ~US$8.3bn in 2020 involving ECAs and 28 banks; further deals later).  
- Classic example of equity/sponsor support early → massive multi-sourced project finance at FID/construction → refinancing once operating.

**Rio Grande LNG (Brownsville, Texas, USA – NextDecade and partners)**  
- Phase 1 (Trains 1–3): Positive FID and concurrent full financing close July 2023.  
  - Total estimated Phase 1 costs ~US$18bn.  
  - Equity: ~US$6.1–6.2bn commitments via JV with GIP (BlackRock), GIC, Mubadala, TotalEnergies + NextDecade.  
  - Debt: ~US$12.3bn including construction term loans (~US$11.1bn), working capital facility (US$500m), and first-of-its-kind US LNG private placement notes at FID (US$700m).  
  - Multi-sourced bank group (MUFG, Mizuho, JPMorgan, etc.).  
- Subsequent phases: Separate FIDs and dedicated financings for Train 4 (2025) and Train 5 (2025/2026). Each ~US$6.7bn with their own equity JV commitments, senior bank facilities, private placement notes (tranched funding), and back-leverage/FinCo facilities for sponsor equity contributions.  
- Illustrates pure phased development: each major train/phase gets its own capital stack once offtakes and readiness support FID.

**Other notes**: Hybrid RBL/project finance structures used in various offshore/African projects (e.g., references to Offshore Cape Three Points, Ghana). Bridge facilities common in early development (e.g., Tamar gas field, Israel). Aramco midstream pipeline monetizations involved large investor consortia and subsequent refinancings/sukuk.

### Mining Projects

**Centinela Copper Mine Expansion (Chile – Antofagasta plc and Marubeni)**  
- Landmark ~US$2.5bn project financing (part of ~US$4.5bn expansion for second concentrator and infrastructure).  
- Multi-sourced: Consortium of export credit agencies (Export Development Canada, JBIC, KfW IPEX-Bank, KEXIM, Euler Hermes) + international commercial banks (Crédit Agricole, Natixis, Société Générale, SMBC, etc.).  
- Supports construction and operation; increases copper output significantly. Relies on seawater and renewable energy.  
- Example of large-scale mine expansion financed via classic multi-source project finance (ECAs + banks) at the development/construction stage.

**Generation Mining Marathon Copper-Palladium Project (Canada)**  
- Assembled multi-source capital stack (~C$969m toward ~C$992m initial capex):  
  - Senior debt facility (~C$424m) from EDC, ING, Société Générale (project finance).  
  - Streaming agreement (C$240m) with Wheaton Precious Metals — upfront capital in tranches (part already delivered; remainder progressive through construction).  
  - Subordinated construction debt (C$110m) + standby/overrun from Canadian Infrastructure Bank.  
  - Equipment leasing facilities (~C$145m).  
  - Planned equity raise.  
- Streaming provides non-dilutive capital timed to construction needs; overall stack mixes senior project debt, streams, subordinated/government-linked debt, and leasing.

**Skouries Gold-Copper Mine (Greece – Eldorado Gold / Hellas Gold)**  
- Project financing closed April 2023 (~€680m) after construction had started years earlier (2012) and paused/restarted.  
- Mix: Commercial bank loans (~€480m from NBG, Piraeus Bank, etc.) + Greek Recovery and Resilience Facility funding (~€200m via EU/Greek State).  
- Plus VAT facility and contingent cost-overrun support; limited-recourse with sponsor equity commitment (~20%).  
- Demonstrates financing closing (or restarting) mid-development once conditions align, with blended commercial + public/recovery sources.

**Other mining patterns**:  
- Phased approaches are common (e.g., start with limited high-grade production or heap leach Phase 1 funded by equity/early cash flow, then expand with operating profits or sequential project finance).  
- Milestone-based funding (e.g., development agreements where a partner/contractor funds specific stages such as drilling, detailed engineering, equipment delivery against convertible loan or equity conversion).  
- Early-stage: Equity, farm-ins, offtake-linked prepayments or streams. Construction: Project finance, ECA-backed, streaming. Production: Cash flow, RBL-style or offtake finance, refinancing.

## Summary Takeaways
- Capital is staged and multi-sourced to match declining risk and rising capital intensity.  
- Early = equity/strategic partners/bridges.  
- FID/Construction = large multi-sourced project finance (banks, ECAs, bonds, private credit, streams).  
- Production = cash flow, RBL, refinancing, production payments.  
- Phased projects (multiple trains, mine expansions, sequential field phases) often have repeated or sequential financing events.  
- Public examples like Ichthys, Rio Grande LNG, Centinela, Marathon, and Skouries illustrate real-world application across oil/gas and mining.

## 5. Stage-by-Stage Capital Inflows – Clearer Breakdowns for Key Examples

Public projects rarely publish exact dollar amounts spent in every sub-phase of exploration/appraisal, but the following show the typical pattern of *when* major capital is committed or drawn.

### Rio Grande LNG (NextDecade) – Clearest modern multi-phase example

**Phase 1 (Trains 1–3, ~US$18 bn total project cost)**  
- **Pre-FID / Development & FEED / Limited Notice to Proceed**: NextDecade invested ~US$125 million of its own capital (part of its total ~US$283 m equity commitment). This covered site work, engineering, regulatory, and early EPC limited-notice costs.  
- **At FID (July 2023)**: Full capital stack closed simultaneously.  
  - Equity commitments: ~US$6.2 bn total (GIP, GIC, Mubadala, TotalEnergies + NextDecade’s remaining commitment).  
  - Debt: ~US$12.3 bn (US$11.1 bn construction term loans + US$500 m working-capital facility + US$700 m private-placement notes).  
- **Construction**: Debt is drawn in tranches against construction progress / milestones. Remaining equity contributions are called as needed. Later refinancings of portions of the bank debt into longer-term notes occurred once construction advanced.  
- **Production**: Operating cash flow + any residual refinancing.

**Later trains (Train 4 and Train 5, each ~US$6.7 bn)**  
Each had its own independent FID + full financing close (2025).  
- Equity JV commitments (NextDecade + partners) closed at FID.  
- Senior bank facilities and private-placement notes closed at FID.  
- NextDecade financed its equity share via separate corporate-level “FinCo” and “Super FinCo” term loans (back-leverage) so it did not dilute common shareholders.  
- Notes for Train 5 issued in multiple installments after FID (e.g., progressive draws in 2025–2026).  

This is the cleanest public illustration of “money arrives at FID for each phase, with some sponsor pre-FID spend and subsequent construction drawdowns.”

### Ichthys LNG (INPEX / TotalEnergies et al.)

- **Exploration / Appraisal / Early development (pre-2009–2011)**: Primarily sponsor equity and internal cash. Inpex raised capital via a public offering in the period leading into FID to prepare for the large commitment.  
- **FEED (~2009 onward)**: Sponsor-funded.  
- **FID (January 2012)**: Decision to proceed.  
- **Financial close (December 2012 / January 2013)**: ~US$20 bn multi-sourced senior debt package (ECA direct loans, ECA-covered commercial loans, uncovered commercial bank loans, senior sponsor loans) + equity commitments. Equity is injected pro-rata or as required to maintain debt-to-equity ratios during the drawdown period. First drawdown occurred shortly after close (February 2013).  
- **Construction (2013–2018)**: Progressive drawdowns of the debt facilities matching construction spending and milestones.  
- **Production (from 2018) + Financial completion (2019)**: Operating cash flow; large subsequent refinancings of the original project debt (e.g., ~US$8.3 bn in 2020).

### Generation Mining Marathon (Copper-Palladium, Canada)

- **Pre-construction / Feasibility / Permitting**: Equity raises and early streaming. Wheaton Precious Metals streaming agreement (originally 2021) delivered an initial C$40 million upfront.  
- **Construction decision / Financing close (2026)**:  
  - Senior project-finance debt ~C$424 m (EDC, ING, Société Générale) – available for construction.  
  - Remaining streaming capital ~C$200 m – progressive through construction phase.  
  - Subordinated debt + overrun facility from Canadian Infrastructure Bank (~C$200 m total).  
  - Equipment leasing facilities ~C$145 m.  
  - Additional equity still being finalized for the full package.  
- Drawdowns of debt and remaining stream payments are milestone- or progress-linked during construction.

### General Pattern Across Public Projects
| Lifecycle Stage              | Typical Capital Type & Timing                                      | Example Amounts / Notes |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Exploration / Appraisal     | Sponsor equity, farm-outs, seed/PE capital                         | Usually small relative to total; rarely disclosed in detail |
| FEED / Pre-FID development  | Sponsor equity, limited bridges, early streams or offtake prepayments | Rio Grande Phase 1: ~$125 m NextDecade pre-FID |
| FID / Financial Close       | Large equity commitments + multi-sourced senior debt closed        | Rio Grande Phase 1: $6.2 bn equity + $12.3 bn debt at FID; Ichthys $20 bn debt package at close |
| Construction                | Debt drawn in tranches vs milestones; remaining equity called; streams progressive | Most of the large debt quantum is utilized here |
| Production / Operations     | Operating cash flow, RBL, production payments, refinancings        | Ichthys large refinancings post-financial completion |

The dominant pattern is: **limited sponsor capital early → large committed multi-source package locked at FID/financial close → progressive drawdowns during construction → cash-flow / refinancing once producing**. Phased projects simply repeat the FID + full financing event for each major increment.

## 6. Pre-FID Capital Structures (Early & Mid-Stage) – Detailed Answer

### Definitions (clarified)
- **Early stage**: Exploration + Appraisal. Finding hydrocarbons and determining if they are commercial. Highest technical risk.
- **Mid-stage / Pre-FID development**: Concept selection → Feasibility → Pre-FEED → FEED → Permitting → Commercial structuring (offtakes, contracts). Still before FID. Risk lower than pure exploration but still too high for classic senior project debt or RBL.
- **Midstream** (different concept): The industry segment of pipelines, processing, storage, LNG plants, etc. A midstream *project* can itself go through early/mid/late development phases.

Pre-FID capital is almost always **much smaller** than the full project cost funded at FID (often single-digit percent, or low tens to low hundreds of millions even for multi-billion projects).

### Typical pre-FID deal structures
Almost pure equity or equity-like. Traditional banks rarely provide significant senior debt or RBL before FID because there are insufficient proved producing reserves and the project is not yet “bankable.”

Common tools:
- Sponsor equity / balance-sheet cash
- Joint-venture cash calls under a JOA
- Farm-outs / farm-ins (partner pays for work in exchange for equity interest)
- Private equity or specialist exploration funds
- Convertible notes or mezzanine (occasional)
- Early streams or royalties (more common in mining)
- Limited Notice to Proceed (LNTP) payments to EPC contractors (still funded by sponsors)

### Specific public disclosures of pre-FID spend
- **NextDecade – Rio Grande LNG Phase 1**: Explicitly disclosed ~**US$125 million** of pre-FID capital investments (part of NextDecade’s total ~$283 m equity commitment). This covered development costs, engineering, regulatory work, and limited-notice-to-proceed work under the Bechtel EPC contracts before the July 2023 FID.
- **Rule-of-thumb industry observation**: One consultancy note indicated that roughly **6% of total Capex** can be spent pre-FID on large LNG projects (e.g., on a $30 bn project that would imply ~$1.8 bn). Actual numbers vary widely and are often not fully broken out.
- **INPEX – Ichthys LNG**: Sponsors funded exploration, appraisal, and FEED themselves. INPEX raised a large corporate equity offering (reported up to ~¥520 billion / ~US$6 bn equivalent in 2010) in the period leading into FID to strengthen its balance sheet for the project commitment. Exact project-level pre-FID spend is not itemized publicly in the same way as NextDecade’s figure.

### Are there pre-FID examples where money is provided *without* exchanging equity?
**Yes.** These are non-dilutive (or minimally dilutive) structures. They do not involve issuing new company shares or selling project equity interests at the time of funding.

**Main examples:**
1. **Commodity prepayment / prepaid offtake agreements** (most relevant for oil & gas)  
   A trader or buyer pays cash upfront (or in tranches) in exchange for the right to receive future production (oil, gas, or LNG) at agreed terms. Repayment is in physical product or from sales proceeds. No equity is issued and no working interest is sold.  
   - BW Energy: ~US$80 million upfront prepay from a trader for Golfinho field development (Brazil, 2023).  
   - GeoPark: US$300 million (expandable to $500 m) from Vitol linked to production from Colombia assets.  
   - Zephyr Energy (2026 LOI): Up to US$15 million non-dilutive pre-production financing via Prepaid Commodity Purchase Agreement for its Paradox project — explicitly no new equity and no sale of asset-level working interests.  
   - Historical example: Alcoa made a large upfront prepayment (reported US$500 million scale) against future gas supply.

2. **Streaming / royalty agreements** (very common in mining, less frequent but used in oil & gas)  
   Investor pays upfront cash for a percentage of future production (or revenue) at a fixed/discounted price. No equity is given. Generation Mining’s Wheaton stream (initial tranche already delivered pre-construction) is an example of this style of capital.

3. **Sponsor-level (corporate) financing**  
   The parent company borrows or uses its own cash/corporate facilities to fund pre-FID work. This does not dilute project-level equity or issue new shares in the project company. NextDecade used corporate resources and later back-leverage loans for its equity contributions.

4. **Other**  
   - Government grants, tax incentives, or development funding in some jurisdictions.  
   - Vendor financing or deferred payment terms from contractors (effectively postpones cash outflow).  
   - Carried interests can be structured so one party funds work and only earns equity later upon success, but pure “no equity ever” is rarer.

**Summary**: Pre-FID capital is smaller, equity-dominated, and higher-risk. Non-dilutive options exist mainly through offtake prepayments, streams/royalties, and corporate-level funding. These become more common when equity markets are difficult or sponsors want to avoid dilution before the project is de-risked at FID.

## 7. Equity vs Non-Equity Deal Structures (Focus: Mining + Conventional Oil & Gas)

### Important Regulatory Note
This is not legal advice. Whether a person can receive a transaction-based fee without being a registered broker-dealer depends on the specific facts of each deal, the nature of the instrument, how the introduction and compensation are structured, and applicable securities laws (U.S. federal and state, or equivalent in other jurisdictions). Pure commercial commodity contracts (many streaming, royalty, and prepaid offtake arrangements) are often viewed differently from equity or securities raises, but the characterization is fact-specific. Anyone considering fee arrangements should consult qualified securities counsel.

### Equity-Raising Deal Structures (typically more likely to raise broker-dealer issues if fees are success-based)
- Issuance of new shares or units in the company.
- Sale of project-level equity / working interest.
- Convertible notes or convertible bonds (these are generally securities).
- Joint-venture equity contributions that involve transferring ownership percentages for cash.
- Typical result: Ownership is diluted; the capital provider becomes a shareholder or working-interest owner.

### Non-Equity / Non-Dilutive (or minimally dilutive) Deal Structures
These generally do **not** involve issuing company shares or transferring project equity/working interest at the time of funding:

**A. Streaming Agreements (especially mining)**  
- Upfront cash (often staged) paid by the streamer in exchange for the right to purchase a percentage of future metal production at a fixed or discounted price.  
- No shares issued; no working interest transferred.  
- Examples: Generation Mining – Wheaton stream (C$240 m, initial tranche pre-construction); numerous Wheaton, Franco-Nevada, and other early-deposit or construction-stage streams; KGL Resources – Wheaton stream; Spanish Mountain – Wheaton royalty-style staged funding.

**B. Royalty Agreements (mining-focused)**  
- Upfront cash for a percentage of future revenue or net smelter return (NSR).  
- No equity issued.  
- Examples: Franco-Nevada royalty packages on development projects (e.g., i-80 Gold $250 m NSR, Minerals 260 / Bullabulling royalty increase + funding, various Franco-Nevada and others).

**C. Commodity Prepayment / Prepaid Offtake (especially conventional oil & gas)**  
- Trader or buyer advances cash against future physical production. Repayment in product or from sales proceeds.  
- Explicitly no equity and no working interest sold in properly structured deals.  
- Examples: BW Energy Golfinho (~US$80 m prepay); GeoPark–Vitol (US$300 m initially, later expanded); Zephyr Energy Paradox Basin LOI (up to US$15 m non-dilutive prepaid commodity purchase); Gran Tierra–Trafigura prepay.

**D. Carried Interests**  
- One party funds the work program; the other is “carried.” An underlying equity or working-interest relationship usually exists even if no cash is paid for the equity at that moment. Not a pure “no equity exchanged” structure.

**E. Convertible Notes**  
- Hybrid. Cash is provided as debt that can convert into equity later. Generally treated as securities.

### Demand for Streaming / Royalty Deals
Yes — demand remains strong and active.  
Major streaming and royalty companies (Wheaton Precious Metals, Franco-Nevada, Royal Gold, Triple Flag, and others) continue to deploy significant capital. Recent years (including 2025–2026) have seen record or near-record deal volumes, including multi-billion-dollar transactions and continued activity with both junior developers and larger producers. Streaming/royalty capital is particularly attractive to mining companies when equity markets are selective or expensive, and when developers want to fund construction or advance projects without further dilution. Private capital is also increasingly active in the space. The model is well-established and continues to expand, including into base metals and critical minerals alongside the traditional precious-metals focus.
