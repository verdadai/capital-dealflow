# Broker-dealer requirements (USA) — junior mining private placements

**Audience:** introducing / advisory intermediary focusing on private placements for junior miners (not holding customer funds).

**Official starting points**
- SEC: [Guide to Broker-Dealer Registration](https://www.sec.gov/about/offices/oia/oia_investman/rplook-guide.htm) / broker-dealer registration materials on SEC.gov
- FINRA: membership / New Member Application (NMA), CAB rules, Rule 5123 private placement filings
- FINRA CAB amendments: [Regulatory Notice 26-04](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-04) (effective March 25, 2026)

## 1) There is no “mining broker-dealer” license

You register as a **broker-dealer** (or elect **CAB** status) and specialize the business plan / Membership Agreement around:

- private placements for natural resources / mining issuers
- institutional (and, if traditional BD, accredited) investors
- corporate advisory / change-of-control work if CAB-permitted

Specialization shows up in WSPs, due diligence files, and marketing — not in a separate license type.

## 2) When registration is triggered

High-risk fact pattern for an unregistered finder:

1. Engaged in the business of effecting securities transactions for others
2. Soliciting investors / negotiating terms / pitching the deal
3. Receiving **transaction-based compensation** (success fee / % of capital raised)

If all three are present on a securities raise, assume BD registration (or affiliation with a registered BD) is required unless counsel says otherwise.

**Usually lower BD risk (still get counsel):** introducing a borrower to a **commercial bank** for a loan/RBL where no security is being sold through you.

## 3) Two structures that fit this model

### Option A — Capital Acquisition Broker (CAB)

FINRA CAB framework is built for limited activities such as:

- acting as placement agent / finder on **unregistered** securities sold to **institutional investors**
- change-of-control / M&A intermediary work for privately held companies (subject to CAB rules)
- corporate advisory on capital raising / restructuring

**Fit for this business:** strong if the investor universe is institutional (funds, QIBs, qualified purchasers, banks, etc.), not broad retail accredited angels.

**Key limits:** CAB is **not** a full-service BD. No customer trading accounts, no carrying customer funds/securities, no market-making, etc.

**2026 updates (RN 26-04, eff. Mar 25, 2026):** CAB rules expanded in ways that matter for placement agents, including broader institutional / “eligible employee” concepts, more flexibility to act for institutional buyers in some contexts, and limited ability to receive issuer securities as compensation under constraints. Confirm current CAB Rule 016 activity list with counsel before relying on any blog summary.

### Option B — Traditional BD, private-placement–only Membership Agreement

Standard FINRA member BD with activities limited to private placements.

**Fit:** needed if you want to solicit **accredited retail / HNW angels** who are not “institutional investors” under CAB definitions.

**Tradeoff:** broader FINRA rulebook and heavier ongoing compliance than CAB.

### Option C — Affiliate with an existing BD/CAB (fastest)

Individual becomes a registered representative of a platform BD/CAB that already has WSPs, FINOP, and supervision.

**Typical exams path**
1. Pass **SIE** (no sponsor required)
2. Get BD/CAB sponsor → Form U4
3. Pass role exam:
   - **Series 82** — private securities offerings (common for placement work)
   - and/or **Series 79** — investment banking
   - and/or **Series 7** — broader
4. Pass **Series 63** (state agent) as required
5. Register in each state of solicitation

This is usually the right first step before spending $30k–$65k+ standing up a firm.

## 4) Standing up your own firm (high level)

If building a firm (not just affiliating):

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Entity | Form LLC/Corp; ownership disclosures |
| Form BD | File via CRD; SEC + SRO registration path |
| FINRA NMA | Business plan, WSPs, supervisors, financials; review can take months |
| Principals | Generally need registered principals (e.g. Series 24) + FINOP (Series 27) model depending on firm type |
| SIPC | Membership as required for BD |
| Net capital | Introducing / non-carrying firms often under the lower SEC Rule 15c3-1 tiers (commonly discussed as **$5,000** minimum for certain limited introducing firms — confirm current category with FINOP/counsel) |
| Fidelity bond | Required bonding for FINRA members |
| State “blue sky” | Firm + agent registration where you solicit |
| Ongoing | AML program, books/records, CE, private placement filings |

**Budget ranges from secondary sources** (treat as planning only): often cited launch runway ~**$30k–$65k+** including legal/compliance consultants, plus monthly outsourced FINOP/CCO. Get a firm quote.

## 5) Mining-specific compliance overlays

Junior mining is high-scrutiny. WSPs should explicitly cover:

1. **Technical report review** — SK-1300 (SEC) and/or NI 43-101 (Canada) as applicable  
2. **Title / mineral rights / surface rights** chain  
3. **Permits & environmental** status  
4. **Stage honesty** — exploration vs PEA vs PFS vs DFS; no overselling “bankable”  
5. **Use of proceeds** tied to technical milestones (drill meters, studies, construction)  
6. **Related-party / promoter** conflicts  
7. **Jurisdiction risk** (sanctions, corruption, offtake country risk)  
8. **FINRA Rule 5123** — file PPM/term sheet materials with FINRA as required (generally within **15 calendar days of first sale** for covered private placements; confirm current rule text)

## 6) Practical recommendation for this intermediary

| Activity | Preferred path |
|---|---|
| Bank RBL / project debt intros | Engagement letter; no securities placement; counsel review |
| Junior miner equity / note PPMs to institutions | CAB or BD affiliation; Series 82/79 path |
| Same to retail accredited angels | Traditional private-placement BD (not CAB-only) |
| Success fee on securities | Only through registered channel |
